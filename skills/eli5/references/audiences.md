# Audiences

Who might be listening, grouped the way Young AI Leaders Linz actually meets people. Each profile says what the listener needs, what loses them, and what to lead with.

The governing rule: **if a sentence would embarrass us in front of an institutional reader, it does not ship**, even if another listener would enjoy it. Precision serves every listener at once. A ministry official and a 22-year-old builder are both checking whether we are serious, and neither is impressed by adjectives.

---

## 1 · Institutional listeners

Ministries, the Federal Chancellery, EU programme and alliance officers, UN and ITU ecosystem contacts, university leadership, foundation and grant officers.

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ministry or public official** | What changes, for whom, from when; the policy lever | Hype, vagueness, any implied endorsement | The policy-relevant consequence in one sentence | Legislation, public services, procurement, infrastructure |
| **EU programme officer** | Fit with the programme's aims, replicability, measurable outcome | Unscoped figures, inflated labels | The outcome and who else can reuse it | Programme logic: objective → activity → result |
| **University leadership** | Academic credibility, student benefit, institutional risk | Student-club tone | What it means for research, teaching or students | Faculties, curricula, research grants |
| **Grant or foundation officer** | Evidence, scope, how the money turns into results | Projections, unverified claims | The result already achieved, then the mechanism | Budgets, milestones, reporting |

Register: flatter, more formal, heavy on named bodies and dates. Still short sentences and strong verbs. German with *Sie* for Austrian public bodies.

## 2 · Partners, sponsors and executives

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Executive / C-level** | Business impact, risk, cost, time | Mechanism before outcome | The one-line takeaway and the decision it implies | Markets, competition, operations |
| **Sponsor or prospective partner** | Whether association is safe and what they plug into | Inflation, unclear asks | What it means for them, then the specific offer | Their own product, customers, talent pipeline |
| **Manager** | Impact on the team, timeline, what decision is needed | Implementation detail | What changes and what you need from them | Projects, staffing, deadlines |
| **Product manager** | User value, scope, priority | Technical rabbit holes | The user problem it touches; build or skip | User journeys, backlogs |

## 3 · Prospective and current members

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prospective member** (a builder, researcher, founder or professional around 18–30) | The real work, the bar, who they would stand beside | "Join our community" language, mailing-list feel | The work itself, then what it demands | Projects they have shipped, studies, startups |
| **New member in onboarding** | How we work and why; what to do first | Jargon and internal acronyms with no gloss | The practical point, then the reason behind it | Their first task, their team |
| **Member in another team** | What it means for their work | Detail they will not use | What they need to know to work with you | The shared project |

Note: the verb for prospective members is always **apply**, never join, sign up or register. Members are selected against the CAR bar: Capability · Availability · Responsibility.

Register: direct and specific for prospective members; plain, practical and warm for current members.

## 4 · Schools and AI literacy

Our AI literacy work is with pupils around age 13. **Capability, not enthusiasm**: the aim is that they can judge AI better afterwards, not just that they like it more. Use media and information literacy framing: where information comes from, what a model does and does not know, how to recognise synthetic content.

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pupil, ~10–14** | A concrete picture and one skill to keep | Lecturing, hype, talking down | A thing they already do (search, chat, scroll) | School, group chats, games, homework, football |
| **Teacher** | Something they can run without us; the learning outcome | Tech evangelism | What pupils will be able to do afterwards | Lessons, assessment, curricula |
| **Parents of pupils** | Safety, usefulness, what their child is doing | Jargon, scare or hype | What the child learns and why it protects them | Household tech, online safety |
| **School authority** | Fit with curriculum and rules; who is responsible | Vagueness on data and consent | The outcome and the safeguards | Curricula, regulations |

Rules for this group:

- **German** for Austrian school materials and delivery, with *du* for pupils and *Sie* for teachers, parents and authorities. A native speaker reviews it before use.
- Never name, quote or describe an identifiable pupil.
- Include one line on what AI can get wrong or how to check it.

## 5 · Public stages and media

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Panel or keynote audience** (Davos, Geneva, a convening) | One idea they will repeat afterwards | Slides of adjectives, unsourced numbers | A named, concrete example | Shared public experience |
| **Journalist** | Facts, a quotable line, the named source | Spin, figures without scope | The fact, then the quote | Their beat |
| **General public / LinkedIn reader** | Why it matters to them, quickly | Buzzwords, hype | The specific thing first | Everyday life, work |

On stage: if asked something you cannot source, say you will follow up. Never estimate.

## 6 · Peers and technical listeners

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Engineer / developer** | Mechanism, architecture, trade-offs | Over-explaining basics, missing terminology | How it works and what is unusual | Systems they know ("like X, except …") |
| **Researcher / grad student** | Nuance, assumptions, limits, edge cases | Imprecision | The claim and its conditions | The literature |
| **Designer** | What the user sees, feels and can do; accessibility | Backend detail | The effect on the experience | Interfaces, flows |
| **Another hub lead** | Replicable detail they can run in their city | Linz-specific context they cannot use | What to copy and what to adapt | Their own hub |

Never speak for the global community or another hub.

## 7 · Ages

| Listener | Words | Comparisons from | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **~5** (ELI5 default) | Short, everyday words; short sentences | Toys, snacks, pets, the playground, bedtime | Wonder, from the picture rather than punctuation |
| **~10** | Simple; one or two new words explained | School, sport, video games, building blocks | Curious, cause and effect |
| **~15** | Normal teen vocabulary, some abstraction | Phones, group chats, streaming, games | Relaxed, never trying too hard to sound young |
| **20s–30s** | Plain adult language | Rent, commuting, studying, first jobs | Direct |
| **40+** | Plain adult language, respectful | Running a household, a career, managing people | Unhurried, never condescending |

## 8 · Education levels

| Listener | Pitch |
|---|---|
| Primary school | No jargon, concrete, "it works like …" |
| Lower secondary (Unterstufe) | One or two real terms, each defined; step by step |
| Upper secondary (Oberstufe / Matura level) | Proper terms with a short gloss; moderate complexity |
| University | Technical terms with brief context; theory linked to a real case |
| Graduate / expert | Assume the basics; spend the words on nuance, trade-offs, limits |

## 9 · Family and friends

| Listener | Tone | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|
| **Partner** | Warm, conversational | Shared routines, home life, plans together |
| **Parents / grandparents** | Patient, respectful, never condescending | Technology they already use: phone, online banking, the post, the kitchen |
| **Kids** | Playful, short | Cartoons, games, school, animals |
| **Friend** | Casual, a little humour | Pop culture, shared hobbies, "you know when …" |

A common one: explaining what Young AI Leaders Linz *is* to family. Use the affiliation chain in plain words, and never let it become "you work for the UN". The correct picture is: a Linz association that is one hub in a global youth community, which sits inside the AI for Good initiative.
