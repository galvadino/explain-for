# House style

A condensed version of the Young AI Leaders Linz voice and writing rules, applied to explanations. When the knowledge base is in the working directory, `docs/03_BRAND/voice.md`, `docs/03_BRAND/terminology.md` and `docs/LAWS.md` win over this file.

---

## Voice

**High-energy, low-hype: charged and exact.** Feeling targets: alive, ambitious, credible, with *serious* as the floor.

| Hype | Charged and exact |
|---|---|
| "AI is transforming healthcare in groundbreaking ways." | "A WhatsApp assistant for diabetes and hypertension prevention in The Gambia, built to WHO protocols, answers from its sources instead of guessing." |
| "Retrieval-augmented generation unlocks the power of your data." | "Before answering, the model looks up the relevant pages in your own documents and answers from those." |

- **Lead with the specific**: a named body, a number, a shipped thing, before any framing.
- **Strong verbs, short declaratives**: build, ship, check, look up, decide, convene, represent. Not "is committed to", "aims to", "strives to".
- **Pace over padding**: rhythm and concreteness, not intensifiers.
- **Global language, local grounding**: where it fits, pair the international with a Linz or Upper Austria specific.

## Banned

- **Superlatives**: premier, leading, world-class, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, best-in-class, biggest, first, only, #1, top, vibrant.
- **Buzzwords**: empowering, unleashing, harnessing, transforming, disrupting, revolutionising, unlocking the power of, the AI revolution, changing the world, making a difference, game-changer.
- **Defining by negation**: "not just a chatbot", "more than a tool", "unlike other …". Say what the thing is.
- **Hedges on strength**: "we hope to", "we're trying to".
- **Announcing the discipline**: never "every number here is sourced".
- **Emoji**, anywhere. **Exclamation marks**, anywhere.
- **"We" meaning the AI field.** "We" is the association.

The German equivalents are banned too: *führend, weltklasse, bahnbrechend, revolutionär*.

## Sentences and structure

- Short declaratives by default; vary length for rhythm.
- One idea per sentence.
- Active voice.
- No rhetorical questions in written copy. A genuine question to a pupil to check understanding is fine.
- Paragraphs of 1–4 sentences.
- Headings are statements, in sentence case. No colon-subtitle headlines, no puns on "intelligence", "smart", "future" or "next".
- Use a list only when the items are genuinely parallel; otherwise write prose.

## Punctuation

| Mark | Rule |
|---|---|
| Em dash `—` | Spaced, for a parenthetical break. The house mark; use it well and not constantly. |
| En dash `–` | Ranges: `27–29 Mar 2026`, `Jun–Dec 2025`. |
| Middot `·` | Tight lists in labels and metadata: `For · Ministry official`. |
| Exclamation mark | Never. |
| Semicolon | Sparingly; a full stop is usually better. |
| Oxford comma | Not used, unless needed for clarity. |
| Quotes | Double quotes for quotations; italics for titles of works and events. |
| Ellipsis | Avoid. |

## Numbers and dates

- Figures in tables and tiles (`9 finalists`); words for small counts in flowing prose ("one of nine finalists").
- Keep `+` exactly as written in the source: `200+ attendees`. Never round, never "almost", "nearly", "over".
- Currency: `€3,000`, `USD 30,000+`. Percentages: `24.6%`.
- Dates: `21 Jan 2025` (no ordinals). Month-year spelled out in prose: `January 2026`.
- Past events carry a date; upcoming events say *upcoming*.
- **Every figure about us carries its scope in the same sentence**: Linz, co-organised, community-wide or global format.

## Names

- **Young AI Leaders Linz** in full in anything public, then "the hub" or "the Linz hub". "The association" in formal contexts.
- "YAIL" is internal only. Never "YAL Linz", "YALL" or "Young AI Leaders" alone for us.
- The affiliation: "the Linz hub of the Young AI Leaders Community, an initiative by AI for Good." Never "UN-backed", "UN partner", "the Austrian chapter".
- **IT:U** is the Linz university; **ITU** is the UN agency. Never mix them up.
- Spell partners exactly as they write themselves: `JKU LIT Open Innovation Center`, `Wirtschaft & Innovation Linz`, `factory300`, `Tabakfabrik Linz`, `neuron.ai Austria`. Spell out in full on first use, then abbreviate.
- Our vocabulary: *member* (not attendee or user), *apply* (not join), *team* (not department), *impact field* (not vertical or pillar), *programme* (British spelling, except in code).

## Temperature by listener

The voice stays the same; the temperature changes.

- **Institutional**: flatter, formal, named bodies and dates.
- **Recruitment**: most direct; says what is required.
- **Social / essay**: more rhythm, a first-person author is allowed, still no emoji or superlatives.
- **Members, family, children**: plain, practical, warm. Warmth comes from patience and a good comparison, not from punctuation.

## Language

English by default. German for Austrian schools and pupils, Austrian public-sector correspondence and regional press. Organisation names stay in English inside German text: *"der Linzer Hub der Young AI Leaders Community, einer Initiative von AI for Good."* German for publication is a draft until a native speaker reviews it.
