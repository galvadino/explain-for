# Listeners

Profiles for the listeners people most often frame for. Each says what the listener needs, what loses them, and what to lead with.

One rule holds for all of them: **precision serves every listener at once.** A minister and a 22-year-old developer are both checking whether you are serious, and neither is impressed by adjectives.

---

## 1 · Decision-makers and institutions

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Minister, public official** | What changes, for whom, from when; the policy lever | Hype, vagueness, overclaiming | The policy-relevant consequence in one sentence | Legislation, public services, budgets, infrastructure |
| **Funder, grant officer, investor** | Evidence, scope, how money turns into results | Projections dressed as results | The result already achieved, then the mechanism | Budgets, milestones, returns, reporting |
| **Board member, executive** | Business impact, risk, cost, time | Mechanism before outcome | The one-line takeaway and the decision it implies | Markets, competition, operations |
| **University or school leadership** | Credibility, benefit to students, institutional risk | Informality, unchecked claims | What it means for teaching, research or students | Curricula, faculties, accreditation |

Register: flatter and more formal, heavy on named bodies, dates and amounts. Still short sentences and strong verbs.

## 2 · Work

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Manager** | Impact on the team, timeline, what decision is needed | Implementation detail | What changes and what you need from them | Projects, staffing, deadlines |
| **Product manager** | User value, scope, priority | Technical rabbit holes | The user problem it touches; build or skip | User journeys, backlogs |
| **Engineer, developer** | Mechanism, architecture, trade-offs | Over-explaining basics, missing terminology | How it works and what is unusual | Systems they know ("like X, except …") |
| **Designer** | What the user sees, feels and can do; accessibility | Backend detail | The effect on the experience | Interfaces, flows |
| **Researcher** | Assumptions, method, limits, edge cases | Imprecision | The claim and its conditions | The literature |
| **Colleague in another team** | What it means for their work | Detail they will not use | What they need to know to work with you | The shared project |
| **Customer, client** | What it means for them; reassurance | Internal jargon | The outcome, in their words | Their own situation |
| **New starter** | How things work here and why; what to do first | Unexplained acronyms | The practical point, then the reason | Their first task |

## 3 · Public and media

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conference or panel audience** | One idea they will repeat afterwards | Slides of adjectives, unsourced numbers | A named, concrete example | Shared public experience |
| **Journalist** | Facts, a quotable line, a named source | Spin, figures without scope | The fact, then the quote | Their beat |
| **General public, social media reader** | Why it matters to them, quickly | Buzzwords, hype | The specific thing first | Everyday life, work |

On stage or in an interview: if you cannot source something, say you will follow up. Never estimate.

## 4 · Learning

For learners, aim for capability: they should be able to do or judge something afterwards. On technology topics, include one line on what it gets wrong or how to check it.

| Listener | Needs | Loses them | Lead with | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pupil, ~10–14** | A concrete picture and one skill to keep | Lecturing, hype, talking down | Something they already do (search, chat, play) | School, group chats, games, sport |
| **Teenager, ~15–18** | Honesty, relevance, some abstraction | Trying too hard to sound young | Why it touches their life | Phones, streaming, part-time jobs, exams |
| **University student** | Theory linked to a real case | Hand-waving | The concept, then the example | Their courses |
| **Teacher** | Something they can run themselves; the learning outcome | Evangelism | What learners will be able to do | Lessons, assessment, curricula |
| **Parents** | Safety, usefulness, what their child is doing | Jargon, scare or hype | What the child learns and why it matters | Household tech, everyday safety |

## 5 · Ages

| Listener | Words | Comparisons from | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **~5** | Short, everyday words; short sentences | Toys, snacks, pets, the playground, bedtime | Wonder, from the picture |
| **~10** | Simple; one or two new words, explained | School, sport, video games, building blocks | Curious, cause and effect |
| **~15** | Normal teen vocabulary, some abstraction | Phones, group chats, streaming, games | Relaxed |
| **20s–30s** | Plain adult language | Rent, commuting, studying, first jobs | Direct |
| **40+** | Plain adult language, respectful | Running a household, a career, managing people | Unhurried, never condescending |

## 6 · Education levels

| Listener | Pitch |
|---|---|
| Primary school | No jargon, concrete, "it works like …" |
| Lower secondary | One or two real terms, each defined; step by step |
| Upper secondary | Proper terms with a short gloss; moderate complexity |
| University | Technical terms with brief context; theory linked to a real case |
| Graduate / expert | Assume the basics; spend the words on nuance, trade-offs, limits |

## 7 · Family and friends

| Listener | Tone | Comparisons from |
|---|---|---|
| **Partner** | Warm, conversational | Shared routines, home life, plans together |
| **Parents, grandparents** | Patient, respectful, never condescending | Technology they already use: phone, online banking, the post, the kitchen |
| **Kids** | Playful, short | Cartoons, games, school, animals |
| **Friend** | Casual, a little humour | Pop culture, shared hobbies, "you know when …" |
