# Voice

Framer's default voice: **clear and exact**. Energy comes from specificity, strong verbs and pace, not decoration. If the project has its own style guide, it wins over this file.

---

## Specific beats impressive

| Hype | Clear and exact |
|---|---|
| "AI is transforming healthcare in groundbreaking ways." | "A WhatsApp assistant answers diabetes questions using only WHO guidance, so its advice stays within the protocol." |
| "Retrieval-augmented generation unlocks the power of your data." | "Before answering, the model looks up the relevant pages in your own documents and answers from those." |

- **Lead with the specific**: a named body, a number, a concrete action, before any framing.
- **Strong verbs, short declaratives**: build, ship, check, decide, fund, present, look up. Not "is committed to", "aims to", "strives to".
- **Pace over padding**: rhythm and concrete nouns, not intensifiers.
- **Local grounding**: where it helps, pair the abstract or international with something the listener can picture nearby.

## Banned by default

- **Superlatives**: groundbreaking, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class, revolutionary, game-changing, leading, premier, unprecedented.
- **Buzzwords**: empowering, unleashing, harnessing, transforming, disrupting, unlocking the power of, the AI revolution, changing the world, synergy.
- **Adjective-stacking** in place of a fact that would prove the point.
- **Defining by negation**: "not just a chatbot", "more than a tool". Say what it is.
- **Hollow hedges**: "kind of", "basically", "sort of", "arguably" when nothing is being qualified.
- **Emoji and exclamation marks**, unless the listener's register clearly calls for them (a child, a casual message to a friend) or the project allows them.

## Sentences and structure

- Short declaratives by default; vary length for rhythm.
- One idea per sentence.
- Active voice.
- No rhetorical questions in written copy. A genuine question to a learner to check understanding is fine.
- Paragraphs of 1–4 sentences.
- Headings are statements, in sentence case. No colon-subtitle headlines, no puns.
- Use a list only when the items are genuinely parallel; otherwise write prose.

## Punctuation

| Mark | Default |
|---|---|
| Em dash `—` | For a parenthetical break. Use it well and not constantly. |
| En dash `–` | Ranges: `7–8 Oct 2026`, `09:30–10:00`. |
| Middot `·` | Tight lists in labels: `For · Your manager`. |
| Semicolon | Sparingly; a full stop is usually better. |
| Ellipsis | Avoid. |

## Numbers and dates

- Figures in tables and tiles; words for small counts in flowing prose when it reads better.
- Copy figures exactly as the source writes them, including `+` and `~`. Never round, and never swap the source's wording for "almost", "nearly" or "over".
- Every figure keeps its scope in the same sentence: whose, where, over what period.
- Dates without ambiguity: `7 Oct 2026`. Avoid `07/10/26`, which reads differently in different countries.
- Past events carry a date; future ones say *upcoming* or give the date.

## Names

- Spell names of people and organisations exactly as their owners do. Spell out an abbreviation on first use, then shorten.
- Use titles as the source gives them.

## Temperature by listener

The voice stays clear and exact; the temperature changes.

- **Formal** (officials, boards, funders, press): flatter, more formal, named bodies, dates and amounts.
- **Work** (managers, teams, customers): direct and practical.
- **Social and essays**: more rhythm, a first-person voice is fine, still no hype.
- **Family, friends, children**: plain and warm; the warmth comes from patience and a good comparison.

## Languages

- Frame in the listener's language. Keep names of organisations, products and events in their original form unless an official translation exists.
- Pick the right form of address: *Sie* or *du*, *vous* or *tu*, and so on.
- The hype ban applies in every language: *bahnbrechend, revolutionär, weltklasse* are out too.
- Text in another language meant for publication or print is a draft until a native speaker reviews it.
